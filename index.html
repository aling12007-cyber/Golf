Kammui Guide to Japan's Premier Golf Destinations

Northern Japan

Hokkaido

Open horizons, cool summers and golf framed by Hokkaido's mountains and forests.

Hokkaido

Niseko Village Golf Course

A grand prize winner at the World Golf Awards, Niseko Village Golf Course is set amongst pretty silver birch forests, with stunning Mount Yotei serving as a cinematic backdrop. Across 18 challenging holes, you’ll experience long manicured fairways peppered with a range of strategic hazards.

Hokkaido

Furano Golf Course

Classic open, lush Hokkaido landscape with two 18-hole layouts including one designed by Arnold Palmer. On rolling hills surrounded by mountain vistas similar to Whistler valley.

Hokkaido

Gozensui Golf Club

A scenic, well-presented, championship-style option; conveniently near Shinchitose international airport. Clear streams, ponds and preserved natural landscape create a tranquil atmosphere that can best be described as Hokkaido’s Augusta. Excellent clubhouse restaurant serves local cuisine.

Hokkaido

A-Brand Golf Club

One of Hokkaido’s premier golf clubs, A-Brand is an 18-hole jewel. It boasts undulating emerald greens, significant elevation changes, tactical bunkers and impressive sea views thanks to the vision of golf course architect Kentaro Sato. A major draw is the opulent clubhouse, inspired by the fairy tale castles of northern Europe. The adjacent hotel serves delicious cuisine featuring fresh local ingredients and seasonal produce.

Pacific coast & Mt Fuji

Shizuoka

Historic courses where coastal drama meets the silhouette of Mount Fuji.

Shizuoka

Kawana Hotel Golf Course

Opened in 1936 and designed by renowned golf architect C. H. Alison, often called the “Pebble Beach of Japan”. Known for steep hills, deep, menacing bunkers, small putting surfaces and spectacular coastal views. It is accessible from Tokyo and regularly ranks among the top 100 golf courses globally.

Shizuoka

Daifuji Golf Course

A historic, traditional 18-hole golf course located in Shizuoka Prefecture. Opened in 1954, during an era lacking heavy machinery, it was crafted largely by hand using picks and shovels, preserving the natural terrain. Designed by agricultural doctor Nobukatsu Maruge, the layout mirrors a traditional Scottish links course. Offers panoramic views of Mt Fuji and Suruga Bay with a timeless, old-school Japan golf atmosphere.

At the foot of Mount Fuji

Yamanashi

High-elevation golf with Mount Fuji not as a backdrop, but as a constant presence.

Yamanashi

Fuji Classic Country Club

A world-class, 18-hole championship golf course located at an elevation of 1,200 meters at the foot of Mount Fuji in Yamanashi Prefecture, Japan. Created by renowned architect Desmond Muirhead. Inspired by Katsushika Hokusai’s famous woodblock print series Thirty-Six Views of Mount Fuji, with each hole reflecting a unique artistic concept. Famous for being the closest golf course to Mount Fuji, offering clear, majestic views of the peak from every single hole, alongside distant vistas of the Minami Alps.

Yamanashi

Narusawa Golf Club

Opened in 1993, this woodland course features wide, largely flat fairways separated by groves of Japanese red pine and white birch. Sitting 1,100 metres above sea level, the course provides cool, refreshing playing conditions during the summer months. Offering Mt. Fuji views with a calm, traditionally Japanese atmosphere.

Southern Japan

Kyushu

Classic clubs, subtropical landscapes and volcanic scenery across southern Japan.

Oita

Beppu Golf Club

One of Kyushu’s oldest clubs (36 holes); the Yufu Course offers dramatic downhill holes and mountain vistas. Tsurumi course has sea views over the Bungo Channel. A classic, historic club atmosphere.

Miyazaki

Phoenix Country Club

Resort-style championship venue; rated top 100 in the world and top 3 in Japan; visitor-friendly but books out especially on weekends; Japan tour pedigree. Direct flights from Seoul.

Kagoshima

Ibusuki Golf Course

Prestigious course designed by Seichi Inoue; on rolling hills at foot of Mt Kaimon, a near perfect volcanic cone rising from the sea. Views of both the mountain and East China Sea give a uniquely Japanese coastal-mountain drama.
